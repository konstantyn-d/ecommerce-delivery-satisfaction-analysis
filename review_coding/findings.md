# Review coding: findings

What customers write when they give 1–2 stars. Based on 300 Portuguese reviews, coded into 8 complaint
categories. All numbers come from [`results.md`](results.md), generated by
[`notebooks/06_review_coding_results.py`](../notebooks/06_review_coding_results.py).

> **Method in one paragraph.** A stratified random sample of 300 reviews was read in the original Portuguese
> and coded with [`codebook.md`](codebook.md), without access to the delivery information of the orders. The
> delivery outcome was joined afterwards. A second coding pass on 30 reviews gave the same primary category in
> all 30 cases; this checks consistency, not validity (see Limitations).

## 1. Three findings

### Finding 1. When an order is late or never arrives, the complaint is that it is not there

| Delivery outcome of the order | Reviews | Complaint is "Not received" | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|
| Late | 77 | 66% | 55–76% |
| Not delivered | 43 | 72% | 57–83% |

Customers of late orders rarely write "it came late" (10% of late-order reviews). They write "I have not
received it". This matches the survey timing found in the data quality review: the survey reaches most late
customers before the parcel does. The bad review on a late order is, in most cases, written while waiting.

### Finding 2. On-time orders with several items: four out of five complaints are about a missing part

| Group | Reviews | Complaint is "Incomplete order" | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|
| On time, several items | 54 | 81% | 69–90% |
| On time, single item | 126 | 8% | — |

This answers the open question from the quantitative analysis, where on-time orders with several items had
3–6 times more bad reviews than single-item orders and the numbers could not say why. Customers describe
receiving one of two units, or one of several products, while the order is shown as delivered. Five reviews in the
sample say explicitly that the website or the carrier marks the order as delivered.

Consequence for the metrics: **these orders count as "on time" in the on-time delivery rate.** The data has one
delivery date per order, so an order with a missing item is recorded as a successful delivery. The on-time rate
of 93.2% therefore overstates what customers experienced.

What the reviews cannot tell: whether the missing part was sent later in a second parcel, or never sent.

### Finding 3. On-time orders with a single item: the complaint is about the product

| Complaint (primary) | Share of the 126 reviews |
|---|---|
| Wrong item | 25% |
| Poor quality or not as described | 23% |
| Damaged or defective | 18% |
| Other or unclear | 11% |
| Incomplete order | 8% |
| Not received | 7% |

74% (CI 66–81%) of these reviews are about what arrived: the wrong article, a damaged one, or one that does
not match the listing. Nine reviews in the sample say the product is not original (a copy or a generic
replacement sold as the branded product). These are seller and catalogue problems, not logistics problems.

## 2. The overall picture

| Complaint (primary) | Share of all 300 reviews | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| Not received | 31.0% | 26–36% |
| Incomplete order | 19.0% | 15–24% |
| Wrong item | 11.7% | 9–16% |
| Poor quality or not as described | 11.7% | 9–16% |
| Other or unclear | 9.3% | 7–13% |
| Damaged or defective | 9.0% | 6–13% |
| Late delivery | 4.7% | 3–8% |
| Seller service and refunds | 3.7% | 2–6% |

Grouped: 35.7% of bad reviews with a comment are about delivery (not received or late), 51.3% about what
arrived (incomplete, wrong, damaged, poor quality), 13.0% about service or unclear.

**Service is rarely the first problem but often the second.** Only 3.7% of reviews are primarily about seller
service, but 21.0% mention it: customers who already had a problem and then could not reach anyone or get a
refund. Service does not create most complaints; it fails to resolve them.

## 3. What changes in the storyline

| Before the coding | After the coding |
|---|---|
| "Late delivery is the main driver of bad reviews" | Still the strongest single driver, and the text confirms the mechanism: customers are waiting, not complaining about a past delay |
| "On-time orders with several items get more bad reviews, reason unknown" | Reason found: incomplete deliveries that the system records as delivered |
| "57% of bad reviews come from on-time orders, content unknown" | They are about the product: wrong, damaged, not as described |
| Recommendation candidates were all about carriers and routes | A second lever appears that carriers do not own: order completeness and seller accuracy |

## 5. Limitations

- **Single coder, no independent second coder.** The consistency check (30 reviews coded twice, same result)
  shows that the rules are applied consistently, not that another coder would agree. The codebook, the coding
  ([`coding.csv`](coding.csv)) and every review text ([`sample.csv`](sample.csv)) are in this repository, so
  each decision can be checked.
- **Only customers who wrote a comment.** The frame is 10,636 reviews; about a quarter of the bad reviews in
  the period have no text and are not represented.
- **300 reviews.** Shares for a single delivery group rest on 43 to 126 reviews; the confidence intervals are
  wide and are shown wherever a share is used.
- **One primary category per review.** Reviews with two problems are assigned to the one that came first;
  the second is recorded as "mentioned".
- **The category "Other or unclear" (9.3%)** contains very short comments and four reviews about parcels that
  were not brought to the door and had to be collected at a post office (six in the whole sample), a theme the
  codebook has no category for.
- **A complaint is a statement at the time of the survey.** "Not received" does not mean the parcel never
  arrived.
